A bet on Ergo's average difficulty

Two people agree on a strike difficulty and a window of time. When the window ends, Ergo's average difficulty over the window decides the result of the bet. Everything is in LIT and on chain, and there is no pool and no price oracle. The difficulty comes straight from Ergo's block headers. All profits and losses come only from the two parties involved in the forwards contract.

1
Offer

A maker picks a side, a strike difficulty, a window of 1 to 120 epochs (about 4 hours to 3 weeks) and a margin, and locks the margin plus 7.5 LIT of fees.

2
Fill

A taker takes the other side and locks the same margin. The window starts at least 2 epochs after the fill to prevent manipulation.

3
Record

Once per epoch, anyone writes the difficulty into the accumulator, a shared on-chain record of the last 128 epochs, and earns 5 LIT for it. Lithos clients do this on their own.

4
Settle

Once the window’s last epoch is recorded, anyone may settle the contract for 5 LIT. Each 1% difference between the window’s average and the strike results in a 5% profit or loss for each party.

Words used here

Epoch
Ergo sets a new difficulty every 128 blocks, about 4.3 hours. Each stretch of 128 blocks is an epoch. Tick Block heights at the top to see blocks instead.
Window
How many epochs to bet over. The average difficulty over the total window of epochs decides the bet.
Strike
The difficulty the window’s average is measured against.
Long and short
Going long wins if Ergo's network difficulty ends above the strike, while going short wins if it ends below the strike.
Margin
Each side’s stake, and the most either can win or lose. A 1% change in average network difficulty results in a 5% gain or loss.
Fees
7.5 LIT from each side: 5 LIT keeps the difficulty record going, 2.5 LIT pays whoever settles. They are not returned.

Who it is for

Miners hedging income. When network difficulty rises, mining rewards fall. Miners who wish to hedge against this risk can go long on difficulty, allowing them to soften their losses.
Miners looking to multiply profits. When network difficulty drops, mining rewards grow. Miners with greater risk appetites can go short on difficulty, potentially gaining more profits than normal from counterparties looking to hedge.
Traders. Price and difficulty are often correlated, but traders and other market participants may find difficulty forwards preferable to bets on Ergo's price alone.

To size a hedge, match the income over the window: 2,000 LIT of margin is a 10,000 LIT notional, so each 1% move pays 100 LIT, about what a 1% change in difficulty does to 10,000 LIT of income.

What to watch

  • Short windows can be pushed. A miner with a tenth of Ergo's hashrate who mines elsewhere for one epoch lowers that epoch's difficulty by about 7.5%. This client caps windows under 8 epochs at 200 LIT of margin.
  • Standing offers can be picked off. Each difficulty change makes a window easier to forecast, and a taker fills only offers that have turned in their favour. New offers expire after one epoch unless you move them, and never after the last block that can fill them.
  • LIT is not hedged. Margins and payouts are LIT, so the LIT price moves what a position is worth in ERG.
  • Settlement is automatic, but open to anyone. Lithos clients record each epoch and settle finished positions. If nobody did for 128 epochs after a window began, each side would get its margin back instead. You can also settle a finished position yourself from Portfolio, and keep the settler's 5 LIT.
  • Offers fill whole. There are no partial fills.
