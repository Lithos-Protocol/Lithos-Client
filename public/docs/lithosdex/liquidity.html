Providing liquidity

Advanced

Assumes you know what an automated market maker is and what price movement does to a liquidity position. Read the LithosDex overview first.

Liquidity providers own the LithosDex pool's reserves and earn the fees every swap pays. This page covers opening a position, the two ways it earns, claiming those earnings, and closing it again. How to place orders in general is in Trading with orders.

What a position is​

A position on LithosDex is a provision: a box of its own on Ergo that records how many shares of the pool you own. It holds no ERG or LIT of yours beyond a fixed 0.6 ERG floor. Your ERG and LIT sit in the pool, and your shares say how much of them is yours.

Each provision comes with an owner NFT, a token minted for that provision alone and sent to your wallet. Claiming the provision's fees and closing it both require the NFT as an input.

The owner NFT is the position

Whoever holds the NFT owns the provision, its fees and its share of the reserves. Sending the NFT to another address hands all of that over. Losing it loses the position, and nothing can recover it.

Opening a position​

A deposit adds ERG and LIT to the pool at its current ratio. To deposit from the web interface, follow these steps:

  1. Open LithosDex, then the Liquidity tab.
  2. Under Add liquidity, enter the ERG to deposit. The interface works out the LIT that matches it at the current price.
  3. Check the quote, including the shares you receive and, for an order, the Minimum shares.
  4. Select Place order, or Deposit liquidity when Use orders is off.

The deposit also funds the new provision box with 0.6 ERG, which comes back to you when you close it. If the pool moves before your deposit lands, whatever does not match the new ratio is returned rather than traded.

As an order, a deposit carries a minimum number of shares. It protects you from a pool someone moved in the same block: a deposit that would buy fewer shares than your minimum does not fill.

How a position earns​

Each swap pays two fees to providers, and they reach you in different ways:

FeeShare of each swapHow you receive it
Pool fee0.15%It stays in the reserves, so each of your shares is worth more when you close
Permanent fee0.15%It is set aside and claimed separately, without closing the position

Permanent fees are kept apart from the reserves, so price movement in the pool does not touch them. What your shares are worth in the reserves does move with the price, as in any automated market maker. The permanent fees you have earned do not.

Claiming permanent fees​

Permanent fees pass through two steps before they reach you:

  1. The pool collects them. Every swap sets its permanent fee aside inside the pool box.
  2. A flush moves them to the vault. The vault is a separate box that pays providers. Miners flush at the end of the order runs they put in their own blocks, and anyone can flush from the Flush tab.

Once fees are in the vault, the Fees tab lists them as Claimable now. Select Claim settled permanent fees on a provision to send them to your wallet. Fees still in the pool are listed as Awaiting flush and become claimable after the next flush.

Each provision is owed its own share of every fee that arrived while it existed. Claiming takes nothing from anyone else, and what you do not claim stays owed to you.

Closing a position​

Closing redeems your shares for their part of the reserves, returns the provision box's 0.6 ERG, and burns the owner NFT. To close from the web interface, open the Liquidity tab and use Remove liquidity.

A redemption forfeits every permanent fee the provision has not claimed. The provision box is spent, and the fees owed to it go with it. The interface handles this for you in each mode:

ModeWhat happens to fees
OrderFees already in the vault are claimed in the same transaction that places the order. Fees still awaiting a flush, and any that arrive while the order waits, are lost when it fills
DirectNothing is claimed. The interface warns you and requires Redeem anyway and give up these fees before it sends

To keep everything, flush from the Flush tab, claim from the Fees tab, and redeem afterwards.

Keeping an idle position alive​

Ergo lets anyone collect a box that has sat untouched for four years. A provision can safely sit much longer than that:

  • Claiming renews it. A claim replaces the provision box with a new one, which starts the clock again.
  • Anyone may refresh an old one. After 525,600 blocks, about two years on mainnet, anyone may replace a provision box with an identical new one. A refresh moves nothing and changes no owner.
  • The 0.6 ERG floor covers the rest. If neither of the above happens, the floor pays for two storage-rent collections, and the provision survives both with a smaller floor.
