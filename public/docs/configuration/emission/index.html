Collateral and emission

Advanced

Assumes you have read how collateral and the permit work in Lithos Protocol.

The emission block covers two different jobs that share a timer. The first is driving the queue, which every client does and which costs only transaction fees. The second is joining the queue with your own funds, which locks ERG and LIT until a miner spends the box, and which ships off.

This is maintenance that runs whether or not you ever mine a block.

A queued position cannot be withdrawn

Once your ERG and permit are in the queue, neither returns until a block spends the box. There is no cancel path, by design. Everything under Lending your own funds is a commitment rather than a setting you can back out of.

Driving the queue​

SettingDefaultRangeWhat it does
emission.enabledtruetrue or falseDrive the collateral queue at all
emission.queueInterval2400001000 to 3600000 msHow often to look at the head of the queue
emission.maxQueueSpends41 to 128Emission spends chained in one pass, which is how deep into the queue a pass reaches
emission.queueScanLimit5001 to 100000Queue boxes paged through when looking for the head
emission.txFee1000000The network minimum fee to 1000000000 nanoERGFee on emission transactions

Activating the head of the queue turns a waiting position into a live collateral box, which is what keeps collateral boxes available for everyone to mine against. Clearing removes a box whose lender key is already active and which would otherwise hold the whole queue still.

Both are permissionless, and clients are encouraged to keep this on so that pool liveness is mainained.

txFee is floored at the network minimum because a transaction below it will not relay.

Chaining off the mempool​

SettingDefaultRangeWhat it does
emission.mempoolChainingtruetrue or falseChain off unconfirmed spends of the emission box rather than waiting for confirmation
emission.maxChainDepth160 to 4096Unconfirmed spends followed before falling back to confirmed state

During a join burst the confirmed emission box is several spends behind the real tip, so without chaining the queue can barely be driven at all.

The unconfirmed spends being chained onto belong to other lenders. They are chained onto to ensure maximum effectiveness and queue slot availability.

Lending your own funds​

emission.maxPermitPerJoin40000000000000Any positive value, in LIT base unitsThe most LIT to post as a permit on one box

What a position costs​

Each live box locks 2.915 ERG plus its permit until a miner spends it. So maxOwnCollateral is your total exposure ceiling, not a throughput setting: at the default of 10 you can have about 29 ERG plus ten permits committed at once.

maxPermitPerJoin is a price you are willing to pay, not a fee. The permit rises with the queue backlog, from a floor of 2,000 LIT toward a ceiling of 40,000. When the thermostat passes your cap, the run stops rather than paying it. The default is the protocol ceiling itself, so out of the box price never blocks a join.

The value is in base units at 9 decimals, so 40,000 LIT is 40000000000000.

Collateral boxes may contain extra rewards

Collateral boxes support fee futures, where lenders may add additional ERG to their collateral box, in hopes of being selected out of the live set quicker. Since transaction fees go to lenders, this can break down into a sort of fee futures market, where lenders may add more ERG to their collateral box in hopes of getting selected when they expect transaction fees to be higher. Fee futures are supported by the client, but no config or API options allows them to be set yet.

One key per box, and why it matters​

The active set holds distinct keys. One live box needs one address, and a key already in the set produces a box nobody can activate.

A reused lender key forfeits the whole box

A queue box whose key is already active can only be cleared, and clearing forfeits both the principal and the permit to whoever builds the clearing transaction. This is why maxLenderKeys may not exceed node.numAddresses, and why the client refuses a manual join while autoCollateralize is on: the automated pass owns key selection and a manual join could take a key it is already using.

Startup checks the pair and names the value to raise:

emission.maxLenderKeys
    64 exceeds node.numAddresses (32). Addresses past index 31 would receive funds this
    client cannot sign for. Raise node.numAddresses to at least 64
