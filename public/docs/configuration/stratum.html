Stratum and difficulty

Standard

For anyone running the Lithos client. No Ergo or smart contract experience needed.

The stratum block controls the local server your mining software connects to, and the difficulty you commit to on chain. This page covers everything under stratum except stratum.candidate, which is large enough to have its own page.

stratum.diff is the most consequential setting in the whole file. It decides both what you are paid and whether you are paid at all.

Difficulty​

SettingDefaultRangeWhat it does
stratum.diff4.0GA parseable difficulty above zero. RequiredThe difficulty you commit to on chain. Your payout share is proportional to it
stratum.diffRefreshInterval600001000 to 3600000 ms. RequiredHow often the client re-reads your committed difficulty from chain state
stratum.forceConfigDifffalsetrue or falseMine at diff instead of your on-chain commitment. For testing only

The format is a number followed by a power-of-ten suffix: K, M, G, T or P. So 4.0G, 1.52M and 300.1K are all valid. Startup parses the value and refuses anything it cannot read or that resolves to zero or less.

Choosing the number is a real decision, covered in the startup guide. In short: your payout is proportional to your committed difficulty, but you earn nothing for a block unless you produced 10 super shares in the 60 blocks before it. Set it as high as you can while still reliably clearing 10 - 15 super shares.

Testing without committing​

forceConfigDiff makes the client mine at the diff in your file rather than at whatever it has committed on chain. Paired with state.autoCommit = false, it lets you measure super share rates at several difficulties without writing any of them to the blockchain:

stratum.forceConfigDiff = true
state.autoCommit = false
Return both to their defaults before mining for real

With forceConfigDiff = true, mining below your on-chain commitment produces NISPs that will not be accepted. The setting exists to measure hardware, not to run on.

The stratum server​

SettingDefaultRangeWhat it does
stratum.stratumPort44441 to 65535. RequiredThe TCP port your mining software connects to
stratum.connectionTimeout600001000 to 3600000 ms. RequiredHow long a silent connection is held before it is dropped
stratum.blockRefreshInterval1000100 to 600000 ms. RequiredHow often the server checks whether it has new work to push
stratum.reduceShareMessagestruetrue or false. RequiredRaise the difficulty reported to your mining software without changing what Lithos pays you at

Share traffic​

At low difficulties your mining software may send enough shares to trigger errors about duplicate or old shares. reduceShareMessages raises the stratum difficulty by a factor of 10,000, so your mining software sends only super shares. The difficulty Lithos evaluates and pays you at is unchanged. Besides the reduced traffic, another benefit is that you can directly see the number of super shares you made in your mining software. With this setting on, all accepted shares are automatically super shares, so it can easily be read in the mining softwares CLI output.

It ships on, and there generally no reason to turn it off outside debugging.

Extra nonce​

SettingDefaultRangeWhat it does
stratum.extraNonce1Size21 to 8 bytes. RequiredHex bytes of extraNonce1 handed to each connection
stratum.rotateExtraNonceInterval00 to 3600000 msHow long a connection may go without submitting work before its extraNonce1 is rotated. 0 disables rotation

extraNonce1 splits the nonce space between connections so two rigs never search the same range. The size trades that split against the space each rig gets to work through in a single job.

Two is recommended and shipped. It leaves each rig a large nonce space for one job. Raising it narrows that space, which invites duplicate shares and extra nonce changes in your mining software.

Rotation exists only for setups that raised the size above 2 and are running out of distinct values. At the shipped size you should not need it, which is why it ships disabled.
