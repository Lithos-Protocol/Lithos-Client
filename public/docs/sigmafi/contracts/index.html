ZC Standard contracts

Expert

Assumes ErgoScript, box registers, context extensions and the Ergo indexed node API.

This page is the developer's reference for the contracts behind SigmaFi: what each one holds, how its spend paths are chosen, and how to prove a bond token is genuine. Each contract's header comment, in the Sigma-Finance-V2 repository, is the authoritative schema. This page explains how the pieces fit together. What clients write on top of the contracts is on The zcs1 off-chain standard, and the pricing formulas are on Pricing.

Where the trees come from​

The Lithos client pins the compiled ErgoTrees of Sigma-Finance-V2 commit adce8ac and never compiles them itself. GET /sigmafi/zc/info returns, for both markets, each contract's tree hash, template hash and address, each expression's hash, and the zcs1 defaults. Read trees from there or from the pinned reference rather than compiling the source: a different compiler version can produce different bytes.

All markets of one contract share a template hash, and each market has one address. The Ergo indexed node looks boxes up by ErgoTree, template hash and token id, but not by register, which is why the contracts are laid out this way.

The contract set​

The set has the following contracts, grouped by folder:

The following are expressions, not box scripts:

ExpressionRole
MaturityLiquidationLiquidation at or after maturity
ERG_SigUSD_LiquidationLiquidation at maturity, or before it below an oracle collateral ratio
ERG_SigUSD_CollateralPolicyA lend offer's risk rule
YieldOrder_Pricing, CurveOrder_PricingA priced order's principal: two-point yield, or a window order's curve

A host box commits to an expression's hash in a register. The spender supplies the serialized expression in a context variable, and the host runs it with executeFromVar. A new rule is a new expression, and no host contract changes. The ERG and SigUSD markets have their own compiled copy of each market-specific contract and expression, DutchAuctionMint included. BondVault, FractionalMint, FractionalVault, DutchAuctionVault, MaturityLiquidation and the wrap contracts are shared by both markets.

Design rules​

Core and edge. The bond and its vault are the core. Every contract that verifies a bond pins their script hashes, so changing them starts a new generation that existing contracts cannot read. Orders, offers, asks and mints are edge contracts. They hold funds only while open, and they meet through conventions rather than each other's hashes, so new ones can be added without migrating anything.

The three-unit rule. A fill mints exactly three units of the bond token. Two stay in the bond, then its vault, and one circulates. The two never split, and the holder of the circulating unit can never assemble two. So for a genuine token X, the box holding two units of X is the loan's state box: the live bond before settlement, the vault after. That is one lookup by token id, and one data-input check on chain.

A live bond can also burn all three units on its extinguish path. Burning three units therefore does not prove the vault was spent. Any contract that holds one unit and has a permissionless pull or sweep path, such as the share vaults, must pin BondVault's hash explicitly. Both share vaults do.

One currency per loan. A loan is lent and repaid in one currency, its market's repayment asset. Every bond has principal and repayment in one unit, so simple yield to maturity is defined for every bond.

Constants for markets, registers for terms. The repayment asset is a compiled constant, so each market has one address. Per-loan terms are registers, so every order of a market shares that address and other contracts can read the terms. Comparisons against the asset constant use a runtime value, because a constant-only test folds at compile time and would split the template.

Nobody is assumed online. Every step a third party can run carries a fee the user committed when posting, at least 0.002 ERG where the contract sets a floor. Every box that waits on a user has an exit a batcher can run without the user's signature.

Order and PricedOrder​

The borrower funds an order with the collateral and an expiry fee. Its registers are the following:

RegisterTypeContents
R4Coll[Long]Borrower terms, 7 to 64 values: 0 repayment, 1 principal nanoERG, 2 repayment ERG floor, 3 maturity height (mode 0), duration of at least 30 blocks (mode 1) or least duration (mode 2), 4 expiry height, 5 expiry fee (at least 0.002 ERG), 6 maturity mode, 7 on a payload only the liquidation rule reads
R5Coll[(Coll[Byte], Long)]Principal tokens: none for ERG, one repayment token for a token loan
R6Coll[Byte]Blake2b256 of the borrower's ErgoTree
R7Coll[Byte]Hash of the liquidation expression
R8Coll[Byte], optionalOrder: hash of a fill guard expression. PricedOrder: Coll[Coll[Byte]] of exactly (pricing hash, guard hash or empty)
R9Coll[Long]PricedOrder only: the pricing payload, at most 64 values

For the oracle rule, R4(7) is the ratio in basis points and R4(8) the oldest oracle accepted, in blocks. The fill guard's ratio is R4(9). In a PricedOrder the principal amounts in R4 and R5 are floors.

The one-currency rule at a fill. An ERG order has no principal tokens, a principal above 0, and a floor no higher than the repayment. A token order has exactly one principal token, the repayment token, and at most 1,000,000 nanoERG in both R4(1) and R4(2).

Fill. The order must be INPUTS(0), so the bond token's id is the order's box id. Exactly three units are minted. The outputs are fixed:

OutputContents
OUTPUTS(0)The circulating unit, to any script the lender chooses. R4 to R6 are its EIP-4 name, description and decimals "0"
OUTPUTS(1)The bond: (asset, 2) then the order's tokens. Value is the collateral plus the lender's fee reserve
OUTPUTS(2)The principal plus the unused expiry fee, to the borrower, with R4 equal to the order's box id

Context variable 1 carries the fill guard when one is named, and the guard runs with the order as SELF. A PricedOrder also needs variable 2, its pricing expression. The bond records the start height, which must lie within 8 blocks of the fill's HEIGHT, and its creation height must equal that start. Maturity must leave at least one block to repay. A window order (mode 2) takes the maturity the lender chooses, from R4(3) up to R9(0) blocks after the start.

Cancel and expiry. The borrower cancels at any height by co-spending a box under their script. From the expiry height, anyone may return the order to the borrower, tagged with its id, and keep the expiry fee. Variable 0 names the refund output. Neither path reads a pricing or guard expression, so a box committed to an unknown expression can still exit.

Bond​

The bond's registers are the following:

RegisterTypeContents
R4Coll[Byte]Originating order id, which is also the token id
R5Coll[Byte]Borrower script hash
R6Coll[Long]Borrower terms, copied from the order, with a priced order's actual principal
R7Coll[(Coll[Byte], Long)]Principal tokens
R8Coll[Byte]Liquidation expression hash
R9Coll[Long](fee reserve, start height, maturity height)

tokens(0) is (asset, 2), and the collateral tokens follow. Collateral ERG is value less the fee reserve. The bond has five spend paths, chosen by context variables:

PathVariablesWhoRule
Repay1, 2Anyone, before maturityVariable 1 names the vault output: (asset, 2) first, R4 the bond's id, R5 0, holding the repayment plus the fee reserve. Variable 2 names the collateral return to the borrower, tagged with the bond's id. With a borrower co-spend the return may hold up to the repayment less of the repayment asset, which is repaying from collateral
Liquidate0, 1Anyone, when the expression allowsVariable 0 is the expression R8 names. It decides eligibility and what the vault receives. The bond pins the vault script, (asset, 2), the R4 tag and R5 1. The fee reserve pays the runner
Recollateralize3The borrower, before maturitySame script, registers and tokens, nothing lower, more of something: ERG, a held token, or a token bond's repayment token. At most once per 60 blocks from the box's creation height, and each successor is stamped within 15 blocks of inclusion
Refresh9Anyone, once the box is over 525,600 blocks oldRe-creates the box unchanged, keeping up to 0.01 ERG as a bounty and never going below the fee reserve plus 0.001 ERG. Stamped 60 to 999 blocks back. Not allowed in the 720 blocks before maturity
Extinguishnone of 0, 1, 3, 9The borrower and the unit's holder, at any heightA borrower-script co-spend, tokens(0) == (R4, 2), more than two units of the asset among the inputs and none among the outputs. Outputs are unconstrained

Every settlement explicitly burns the bond's two units into the vault output. A builder that lets them escape into change violates the bond. Never select a circulating unit as automatic funding for a settlement: no unit of the asset may survive in the settlement's outputs except the two in the vault.

BondVault​

The vault holds (asset, 2) and the settlement. R4 names the bond box that settled, and R5 is 0 for a repayment or 1 for a liquidation. The holder claims by spending the vault with the circulating unit and burning all three units, and chooses the outputs freely. One transaction can sweep many vaults, each by its own unit. Anyone may refresh a vault over two years old with variable 9.

LendOffer​

The offer's registers are the following:

RegisterTypeContents
R4Coll[Byte]Owner script hash
R5Coll[Long]Fixed terms, then up to 20 curve points. ERG offers: 0 least yield, 1 and 2 least and most blocks to maturity, 3 batcher fee, 4 new-bond fee reserve, 5 collateral floor, 6 expiry, 7 least price, 8 oracle age, 9 least auction floor, 10 most auction expiry. Token offers add 11 original capital, 12 ERG budget, 13 least fill, 14 reward budget. Then (yield bps, blocks left) pairs, blocks strictly increasing
R6Coll[Byte]The collateral policy's hash, followed on a continuation by the 32-byte id of the offer box it continued from
R7Coll[Long]Share delivery terms
R8Coll[Coll[Byte]]Share metadata for deliveries into a mint request
R9Coll[Byte]The mint request script a delivery goes into, or empty to deliver the unit

The offer buys from three sources, chosen by context variables: a new bond from an accepted order or priced order, an existing bond unit from any ask, or Selective or Dutch shares from any ask. For an existing unit it checks the origin from the order's serialized box and requires the live bond as a data input. For shares it also checks the mint request's serialized box. Each offer tags its delivery and its continuation with its own input id, so independent secondary purchases can share one transaction. New bonds still need one transaction each, because the mint uses INPUTS(0).

Asks​

Every ask follows one convention, so any lend offer can buy from any ask. An ask sells its tokens(0). R4 is the seller's script hash. A purchase pays one payout box at the seller's script with R4 equal to the ask's box id and R5, a Long, equal to the raw units paid for. A buyer reads the payout, not the ask's price, and requires R5 to equal the units it received.

The two asks' registers are the following:

RegisterSaleOrderPricedAsk
R4Seller script hashSeller script hash
R5(price numerator, price denominator, least fill, expiry)(floor numerator, floor denominator, least fill, expiry, N, then the pricing payload)
R6The ask a partial fill continued fromThe pricing expression hash
R7The bond token id
R8The ask a partial fill continued from

A SaleOrder charges ceil(units × numerator / denominator). The ask box's ERG, 0.001 ERG plus its refund fee, survives partial fills and is not returned separately on a full fill.

LotAuction​

A lot sells everything in its box, whole, to the first buyer, for one currency. R4 is the seller's script hash and R5 is (opening, floor, start height, end height, expiry, refund fee). The price is the opening until the start, falls in a straight line to the floor at the end, and holds until the expiry. On an ERG lot the box's ERG returns to the seller with the payment. On a token lot the box may not hold the currency itself. A lot's payout carries R4 and no R5, so no lend offer can match against a lot.

Shares​

FractionalMint and DutchAuctionMint are request boxes holding one bond unit. R7 is the owner script hash, R8 is (N, pull fee, mint fee) followed on a Dutch mint by the auction terms, and R9 holds the share token's EIP-4 metadata, copied into R4 to R6. Any batcher runs the mint with the request as INPUTS(0), so the share token's id is the request's box id. The mint creates 2N + 1 shares: N to the owner, and N + 1 locked in the vault with the bond unit. The box holding N + 1 shares is the shares' state box.

A Dutch mint's auction terms are an opening and a floor in basis points of the repayment, a length and an expiry in blocks, and an in-kind threshold of at least 100 raw units. A threshold of 100 is Selective. A threshold of Long maximum is Dutch. The mint reads the repayment from the live bond as a data input, or from the bond's serialized bytes in variable 1 when the bond has already settled.

The vaults' paths are the following:

PathWhoWhat it does
PullAnyone, for the pull feeSpends the settled BondVault, verified by its pinned hash, burns all three bond units, and takes the settlement in
Partial claimA holderBurns u shares and takes floor(balance × u / outstanding) of each asset
Final claimA holder of every shareBurns every outstanding share and the reserve and takes everything. Before the pull it returns the bond unit
Auction purchaseAnyoneDutch vault in state 1 or 4 only
RefreshAnyoneAfter two years, as for the bond

A Dutch vault's R6 is its state: 0 repaid, 1 floor auction open, 2 sold or nothing to sell, 3 paid out in kind, 4 excess auction open. R7 holds the auction terms and R8 the auction's currency. The state is fixed at the pull, so nothing later can move an auction from one case to the other.

Wraps​

WrapMint holds the token, the owner hash in R4, the mint fee in R5 and the receipt's EIP-4 data in R6. Any batcher mints three receipt units, whose id is the request's: one to the owner, two and the token into a bare WrapVault. The vault must receive at least the request's value less the mint fee and 1,000,000 nanoERG, so the owner's refresh reserve lands in the vault. The request must be its transaction's only input, so nothing the token has authority over can be spent beside it. WrapVault releases the token only when all three receipt units burn, and its refresh must also be the only input.

Verifying a bond token​

A token X is a genuine standard bond token when both of the following hold:

  1. Origin. The box whose id is X was spent as an accepted Order or PricedOrder. Off chain, fetch that box by id from the indexed node and compare its full ErgoTree. On chain, a contract takes the serialized box in a context variable, checks that its Blake2b256 is X, and checks that the deserialized box carries the order script.
  2. State. The box holding two units of X is the state box. Under the bond script the loan is live and its registers are the terms. Under the vault script it has settled.

A hand-made box under the bond script proves nothing by itself. Always check the origin. Shares go one link further: a share token Y is genuine when the box whose id is Y was spent under a standard mint script holding a genuine unit X. A wrap receipt is verified the same way against WrapMint.

Exits​

Every box that waits on someone has the following way out:

BoxOwner, any timeAnyone
Order, PricedOrderCancelFrom expiry, refund to the borrower for the expiry fee
LendOfferCancelFrom expiry, refund to the owner for the batcher fee
SaleOrder, PricedAskCancelFrom expiry, refund to the seller for the ERG above 0.001 ERG
Share or wrap mint requestCancelThe mint itself, for the mint fee
BondRepay, recollateralize, extinguishLiquidate when the rule allows; refresh after two years
BondVault, share vaultsClaimPull for the pull fee, auction purchases, refresh after two years
WrapVaultUnwrapRefresh after two years, as the only input
LotAuctionCancelPurchase at the current price; from expiry, refund for the refund fee

Refunds are tagged with the box's id, so an owner that is a contract can recognise its funds. Nothing sweeps lost shares or a lost circulating unit: their claims stay stranded.

Cost and size​

The heaviest standard transaction, two offers buying from two priced asks at once, measured 37,345 cost units and 12,376 bytes in the offline harness. That is 3.7% of the node's default per-transaction cost limit. A block fills by size before cost, at about 100 such transactions. A wallet filling an order costs about 20,198 and 2,806 bytes, and a repayment about 20,021 and 2,183 bytes.

Limits and assumptions​

The contracts rely on the following:

  • Each market's constants agree. An order's repayment token must match its bond's, and an offer's hashes must be its market's. Scripts cannot check one another's constants, so clients pin the published set.
  • Liquidation rules, collateral policies and pricing expressions are commitments, not proofs. Both parties inspect them, and a client checks that a bond's rule is the one compiled for its market.
  • The ERG and SigUSD rules trust the USD oracle pool's identity NFT and value SigUSD at $1.
  • Only live bonds can be bought by an offer or priced by a yield ask.
  • A share's value in an offer or a yield ask ignores the share vault's own ERG.
  • Storage rent is outside script evaluation. The paid refresh protects boxes only if someone sends it in the two years after it opens.
  • A capability token put raw into an order, bond or ask lends its authority to whoever spends that box. Wrap it first.
