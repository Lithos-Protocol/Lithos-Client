SigmaFi pricing

Advanced

Assumes you are comfortable with percentages and simple formulas, and have read the SigmaFi overview.

This page gives the exact rules SigmaFi uses to price loans, bonds and shares, with a worked example for each. Every rule here is checked by a contract or an expression on chain, except where it says a client chooses. Which expression a box uses is on Contracts.

Pricing is either fixed or yield. A fixed price is a number the owner commits to. A yield price is worked out at the moment of the trade from the yield the owner commits to.

The yield convention​

Every SigmaFi yield is a simple return in basis points per 262,800 blocks, which is one year of 2-minute blocks. For a bond repaying R with D blocks to maturity, bought for P:

yield = (R / P - 1) × 262,800 / D

The following hold for every yield on SigmaFi:

  • It is simple, never compounded.
  • It is measured in blocks, not calendar time. Ergo's block time varies.
  • It assumes full repayment at maturity. A liquidated bond pays its collateral instead.
  • 100 basis points are 1%, so 1,000 bps is 10%.

Contracts never divide. Each test below multiplies both sides out in BigInt and compares, so the boundary is exact to one raw unit.

Two-point yields​

A yield-priced order or ask commits two points in time:

(start height h0, start yield y0, end height h1, end yield y1),   h1 > h0

The yield is y0 up to h0, y1 from h1 on, and on the straight line between them in between. Equal yields give one fixed rate. Clients put h0 at the current height and h1 at the box's expiry. With:

B  = 10,000 × 262,800
L  = h1 - h0
at = HEIGHT, clamped to [h0, h1]
Y  = y0 × L + (y1 - y0) × (at - h0)          the yield at HEIGHT is exactly Y / L
D  = maturity - HEIGHT
F  = R for a whole bond, or R × units / N for shares

the least principal or payment satisfies:

paid × (B × L + Y × D) ≥ F × B × L          that is,   paid ≥ F / (1 + yield × D / 262,800)

A rising yield makes the price fall while the box waits. A falling yield makes it rise. Any fixed floor the owner set still binds.

Example. A borrower posts at height 1,900,000 with a yield moving from 800 bps to 1,200 bps by the order's expiry at 1,905,040. At height 1,902,520, halfway, Y = 800 × 5,040 + 400 × 2,520, so the yield is Y / L = 1,000 bps, 10%. A bond repaying 110 with 131,400 blocks left then needs a principal of at least 110 / (1 + 0.10 × 131,400 / 262,800) = 104.7619.

Because the price can move between signing and inclusion, a client pays the most the price asks at any height from now to 3 blocks ahead.

Priced orders​

A priced order prices only the principal. The repayment, the collateral, the maturity rule, the liquidation rule and the fees never move. The principal stored in the order is a floor. The bond records the principal actually paid:

  • ERG: the payout's nanoERG, less the refunded expiry fee.
  • SigUSD: the payout's SigUSD exactly. The small ERG beside it stays as committed.

Paying above the minimum is allowed, which gives the lender room for the inclusion height to move.

Fixed or yield: choosing for an order​

How the rate behaves while an order waits depends on its timing and pricing:

TimingPricingWhile the order waits
Term from the fillFixedPrincipal, repayment and duration stay the same, so the rate does too
Term from the fillYieldDuration stays the same. Only a moving yield changes the quote
Fixed maturityFixedFewer blocks remain, so the rate the borrower pays rises
Fixed maturityYieldThe principal follows the fixed repayment at the stated yield as blocks run out

A borrower who thinks in a target rate can work out the repayment: R = P × (1 + yield × D / 262,800), rounded up to a raw unit.

The lend offer's yield curve​

A lend offer holds a minimum yield and up to 20 points of (yield, blocks left to maturity), blocks strictly increasing. For a bond with D blocks left the yield it requires follows these rules:

  • Between two neighbouring points, it is on the straight line joining them.
  • Below the first point it is the first point's yield, above the last it is the last's.
  • It is never below the minimum yield. With no points, the minimum is the one yield.
  • The offer's least and most blocks to maturity bound what it buys at all.

On a segment from (y1, D1) to (y2, D2), with L = D2 - D1 and Y = y1 × L + (y2 - y1) × (D - D1), the offer buys when:

R × B × L ≥ cost × (B × L + Y × D)

For u of N shares, R is replaced by R × u / N. On an ERG offer, the cost includes the batcher fee. The curve is keyed by blocks left, so it rolls with time: a resting offer keeps bidding the same rate for a 30-day bond whatever day it is.

Example. An offer with a 5% minimum and points 6% at 21,600 blocks, 8% at 131,400 and 10% at 262,800 meets a bond repaying 107 with 76,500 blocks left. That is halfway along the first segment, so it requires 7%. The most it pays is 107 / (1 + 0.07 × 76,500 / 262,800) = 104.8632. On an ERG offer with a 0.005 ERG batcher fee, the price itself may be at most 104.8582.

Window orders​

A window order lets the lender pick the maturity, any duration from the borrower's minimum to their maximum. The borrower commits a curve: a cap and up to 20 points of (yield, blocks left). It is the offer's curve read the other way round: each point is the most the borrower pays, and the cap is a ceiling. With D the blocks to the chosen maturity, the yield is the curve's at D, capped, and:

principal × (B × L + Y × D) ≥ R × B × L          that is,   principal ≥ R / (1 + yield × D / 262,800)

The repayment is fixed, so a longer maturity pays the borrower less now. The principal floor still binds at every maturity.

Example. A borrower repays 6, with a 12% cap and points 8% at 21,600 blocks and 12% at 131,400. A lender who picks 43,200 blocks gets a curve yield of 8.787%, so the principal is at least 5.9146. At 131,400 blocks the yield is 12% and the principal at least 5.6604.

When a lend offer fills a window order, zcs1 fixes the maturity a batcher picks. See Batching.

Fees and yields​

A borrower's or seller's yield is the most they offer, which sets a least price. A lender's yield is the least they accept, which sets a most cost. Ignoring fees, an ask offering 10% crosses a bid requiring 8%, and one offering 6% does not.

Fees change that in the following ways:

  • On an ERG lend offer, the cost is the price plus the batcher fee.
  • A SigUSD offer pays its fees in ERG, separately, with a reward per fill proportional to the capital filled and a cap on the total.
  • On a short or small loan the batcher fee can exceed the interest, so equal rates may not match. On a 60-block loan repaying 0.12 ERG, leaving room for a 0.005 ERG fee takes a yield in the tens of thousands of percent.

Share prices​

Shares are quoted as a percentage of face. A price p for u raw shares of a bond repaying R, with N raw shares in all, is:

percentage of face = p × N / (u × R)

Example. 10 shares (10,000,000 raw) of a bond repaying 105.5 SigUSD, offered for 10.20 SigUSD, are at 10.20 × 100,000,000 / (10,000,000 × 105.5) = 96.68% of face.

Auction prices​

Auctions use straight-line prices over blocks, and each price only falls:

AuctionPrice at a height
Lot auctionThe opening until the start, falling in a straight line to the floor at the end, then the floor until the expiry
Share vault, floor auctionA target for the vault's currency: the repayment times a basis-point figure falling from the opening to the floor over the length, then holding. A buyer brings the vault up to the target
Share vault, excess auctionThe opening times the repayment, falling to zero over the length. A sale must pay more than zero

A falling price means a purchase priced now stays valid when it lands, so clients buy at the current height's price.
